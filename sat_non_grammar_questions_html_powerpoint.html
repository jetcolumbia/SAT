Chapter 1 · Transitions

Why transitions matter

  • Transition questions are the most common type of Writing question.
  • Each test contains around eight to 10 transition items.
  • That is around 15-20% of the test.
  • You should be comfortable with both common and less common transitional words and phrases.

What SAT Transition Questions Test

Core skill

  • Recognise when transitions do and do not create logical connections between two sentences or parts of a sentence.
  • Usually, all four answers are grammatically acceptable.
  • The focus is identifying the most logical option in context.

First Move: Identify the Relationship

Do this before looking at choices

  • The blank begins with a transition.
  • Therefore, the correct word must indicate the relationship between the sentence with the blank and the previous sentence or passage portion.
  • Back up and determine how the sentence begun by the blank relates to the information before it.

Eliot Example · Logic Before Choices

Relationship

  • First sentence: "The Waste Land" sold only about 330 copies after publication.
  • Second sentence: Eliot was forced to search for other sources of income.
  • The second statement indicates the result of the first statement.
  • So the correct option must convey result.

Eliot Example · Full Question

Although T.S. Eliot devoted several years to writing "The Waste Land," it sold only about 330 copies in the six months following its publication in 1922. __ Eliot was forced to seek other sources of income. In the mid-1920s, he took a job as the director of a new publishing firm called Faber & Faber.

▣ Mark for Review

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A

Alternatively,

B

Consequently,

C

In fact,

D

Moreover,

Teaching note

Consequently clearly indicates that the second statement describes a result, or consequence, of the first.

Eliot Example · Process of Elimination

Use the function of each transition

  • Alternatively indicates contrast, so it does not fit.
  • In fact emphasises a preceding statement, so it does not fit.
  • Moreover means “in addition” or “furthermore” and introduces a supporting idea, so it does not fit.
  • That leaves Consequently.

Key Takeaway: Function Beats Sound

Why this method is faster

  • Eliminate answers by using the purpose, or function, of each transition.
  • If you know beforehand what purpose the correct word must have, you do not need to plug every option back in.
  • This prevents you from being distracted by how each option sounds in context.
  • It also protects you from vaguely plausible but not-quite-right answers.

Types of Transitions

Three main types

  • Continuers: two sentences express similar ideas.
  • Cause-and-effect words: one action is the result of another.
  • Contradictors: two sentences express different ideas.

Transition Type 1 · Continuers

Continuers

Continuers indicate that two sentences are expressing similar ideas.

Correct: Erenna sirena, a relative of the jellyfish, is one of the rare marine organisms to emit red light. Indeed, only a few other deep-sea creatures produce a glow that colour.

Transition Type 2 · Cause-and-Effect

Cause-and-effect words

These indicate that one action is the result of another.

Correct: The light from most quasars was emitted when the universe was only a fraction of its present age. Therefore, quasars offer an important clue to the appearance of the early universe.

Transition Type 3 · Contradictors

Contradictors

Contradictors indicate that two sentences are expressing different ideas.

Correct: An increased reliance on computerised systems can leave users vulnerable to cyberattacks. However, current defences are stronger than many people realise.

How to Work Through Transition Questions

General rule

Do as much work as you can toward determining the precise relationship before you look at the choices.

  • Reread both sentences or parts of the sentence.
  • Determine the relationship between them, and try to predict the answer.
  • Check the answers, and pick the option that matches.

Predict the Type Before the Word

If you see...

  • Contrast: however, nevertheless, still, in contrast
  • Add similar idea: furthermore, moreover, in addition, similarly, likewise
  • Result: therefore, consequently, accordingly

Also watch for...

  • Example: for example, for instance, specifically
  • Emphasis: indeed, in fact
  • If you cannot define the precise relationship, at least determine the general transition type.

Elimination Rules + Shortcut

Eliminate by relationship

  • If the sentences express similar ideas, eliminate contradictors such as but or however.
  • If they express different ideas, eliminate continuers and cause-and-effect words such as for example or therefore.

Shortcut

If two or more answers contain grammatically identical synonyms, eliminate both because no question can have more than one right answer.

Example: if one choice is consequently and another is therefore, eliminate both.

Antarctica Example · Answer Logic

Opposing ideas

  • Sentence 1: life cannot thrive in Antarctica’s interior.
  • Sentence 2: life can thrive on the Antarctic Peninsula and surrounding islands.
  • These are opposing ideas, so a contradictor is required.
  • Therefore, In contrast is the only possible answer.

Rare Fine Distinction · Same Transition Type

Important caveat

In very rare cases, the College Board has asked students to make a very fine distinction between two transitions of the same type.

This appeared only once on a non-administered exam, but it is worth knowing.

Fine Distinction Example

Conditions in the interior of Antarctica are inhospitable to many forms of life: sub-zero temperatures, high winds, and extreme dryness make it impossible for most animals to survive. __ the Antarctic Peninsula and the surrounding islands have relatively mild temperatures and liquid water, allowing many species to thrive there.

▣ Mark for Review

A

Still,

B

For example,

C

Teaching note

Although still and however are usually interchangeable, however is stronger here.

Still means “despite this,” but the peninsula’s milder weather does not exist despite the harsher weather in the interior. It is simply a different location with a different climate.

Harder Example · Do Not Get Distracted

Focus on the essential information

  • Sentence 1: a few species are able to dwell in Antarctica despite its harsh climate.
  • Sentence 2: lists specific species able to live there.
  • The correct transition must be a continuer signalling that Sentence 2 presents examples of Sentence 1.
  • The only option that matches is Specifically.

Transitions in the Middle or End of a Sentence

What looks like one sentence may test two sentences

Transitions can appear between two commas in the middle of a sentence.

Although that construction may appear to ask about only one sentence, it is usually about the relationship between the sentence containing the transition and the previous sentence(s).

Same Meaning, Different Placement

Version #1

Therefore, tropical marine mammals have very few fat reserves in their bodies.

Version #2

Tropical marine mammals, therefore, have very few fat reserves in their bodies.

Teaching note

The difference is purely stylistic. Both sentences have the same meaning, and in both cases the transition connects the sentence to a previous statement.

Middle-of-Sentence Transition Question

Healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath the skin. Tropical marine mammals, __ have very few fat reserves in their bodies. As a result, many of them dwell primarily in the warm waters that surround coral reefs.

Why the Transition Does Not Connect the Two Halves

Cross it out

If we cross out the transition and split the sentence:

  1. Tropical marine mammals
  2. have very few fat reserves in their bodies

There is no logical relationship between these two fragments when read separately.

What it really connects

It clarifies the relationship between the current sentence and the previous sentence.

Tropical Mammals · Answer Logic

Contrast required

  • Previous sentence: arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat.
  • Current sentence: tropical marine mammals have very few fat reserves.
  • These ideas contrast: thick layer of fat vs. very few fat reserves.
  • However is the contradictor, so it is correct.

Contradictor Placement

1) Healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath the skin. However, tropical marine mammals have very few fat reserves in their bodies.

2) Healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath the skin. Tropical marine mammals, however, have very few fat reserves in their bodies.

3) Healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath the skin. Tropical marine mammals have very few fat reserves in their bodies, however.

Same function

All three have the same meaning: however indicates that the second sentence opposes the first.

End-of-Sentence Transition Question

Tropical marine mammals have few fat reserves in their bodies, __. As a result, they are largely confined to the warm waters that surround coral reefs.

End-of-Sentence Question · Key Point

Placement can mislead you

  • Even though the transition is physically next to the sentence that follows, it still asks about the relationship between the sentence in which it appears and the previous sentence.
  • Those sentences present contrasting ideas.
  • So however is correct.

When the Transition Really Connects Sentence Halves

One-comma construction

You may also be asked to work with a transition in the middle of a sentence that genuinely connects two parts of the same sentence.

These transitions follow only one comma.

One-Comma Question

Tropical marine mammals have few fat reserves in their bodies and dwell primarily in the warm waters that surround coral reefs. In contrast, healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath their skin, __ they are able to tolerate much colder waters.

▣ Mark for Review

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A

so

B

for

C

yet

D

One-Comma Question · Answer Logic

Cross out the transition

  1. Healthy arctic marine mammals have a thick layer of fat beneath the skin.
  2. They are able to tolerate cold water.

The second half is a result of the first, so a cause-and-effect word is required.

Why “so” wins

For can mean “because,” but it reverses the logic. Arctic mammals are not fat because they tolerate cold water; they tolerate cold water because they have fat. Only so creates the correct relationship.

Glossary: Accordingly / Consequently

Accordingly / Consequently = therefore, as a result

Correct: Dolphins are highly social creatures. Consequently, they live in pods of up to a dozen animals.
Correct: Dolphins are highly social creatures. Accordingly, they live in pods of up to a dozen animals.

Glossary: Furthermore / Moreover

Furthermore / Moreover = in addition

Correct: Dolphins are social animals. Furthermore, they are highly intelligent.
Correct: Dolphins are social animals. Moreover, they are highly intelligent.

Glossary: In Fact / Indeed

Used to emphasise a preceding statement

Correct: Dolphins are highly intelligent. In fact, they are one of the smartest mammals.
Correct: Dolphins are highly intelligent. Indeed, they are one of the smartest mammals.

Glossary: Even So / Nevertheless / Still

Despite this, however

Correct: Dolphins are descended from land-dwelling animals. Even so, they can only survive in water.
Correct: Dolphins are descended from land-dwelling animals. Nevertheless, they can only survive in water.
Correct: Dolphins are descended from land-dwelling animals. Still, they can only survive in water.

Glossary: Whereas

Whereas = although

Correct: A salmon is a type of fish, whereas a dolphin is a type of mammal.

Glossary: As Such

As such = as a + noun

As such is one of the trickier transitions.

Correct: Dolphins are social animals. Because they are social animals, they live in pods of up to a dozen animals.
Also correct: Dolphins are social animals. As social animals, they live in pods of up to a dozen animals.
Smoother: Dolphins are social animals. As such, they live in pods of up to a dozen animals.

Glossary: Likewise / Meanwhile

Likewise = similarly, in the same way

Correct: As mammals, dolphins are warm-blooded. Likewise, they nourish their young with milk.

Meanwhile

Literally, “at the same time”; often used to mean however and signal a contrast.

Correct: Many people think of dolphins as fish. Meanwhile, they ignore scientific research, which long ago established that dolphins are actually mammals.

Glossary: Subsequently / That Is

Subsequently = afterward

Correct: In the 1980s, dolphin populations began to decline because too many animals were caught in fishing nets. Subsequently, fishing companies began taking steps to reassure customers that products were “dolphin safe.”

That is = in other words

Introduces a definition or explanation, and often follows a dash.

Correct: Dolphins are mammals - that is, they are warm-blooded and nourish their young with milk.

Chapter 2 · Specific Focus (“Student Notes”)

What note-taking questions look like

  • Created specifically for the digital SAT.
  • They usually contain 3-5 bullet points about a researched topic.
  • The question asks you to identify the statement that best uses information from the notes to introduce or emphasise a particular idea.

Student Notes · Big Strategy

Do not be intimidated

  • These questions may look complicated because they contain a lot of information.
  • But only a small fraction of the information is relevant.
  • The answer will virtually always be contained in the question itself.
  • The correct choice simply rephrases the specific focus.

Student Notes · Specific Focus

Look for what the student wants to do

  • Every note-taking question gives you a specific focus: what the student wants to present, introduce, specify, or emphasise.
  • The information after that instruction is the key word or phrase.
  • When checking answers, look for the option that matches the focus indicated.
  • Only one option will fit; when you find it, you are done.

Student Notes · Important Reassurance

No hidden trick

  • All answer choices will refer to information that is actually in the notes.
  • You do not need to worry about answers that have the right focus but are absent from the left-hand column.
  • That kind of trickiness is not the point of the test.

Focus Type 1 · Comparisons and Contrasts

Similar or different?

These questions ask you to identify a statement indicating that two people or things are either similar or different.

Depending on the emphasis, correct answers include words such as like, unlike, or whereas.

Focus Type 2 · Familiar and Unfamiliar

Audience matters

These questions ask you to present a person, artwork, event, etc. to an audience that either has or does not have previous knowledge of it.

  • Correct answers for familiar audiences do not contain background information about the person or thing.
  • Correct answers for unfamiliar audiences contain basic background or historical information and may be longer than other answers.

Similarity Example · Work Without Notes

▣ Mark for Review

The student wants to present a similarity between dinosaurs and modern birds. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to achieve this goal?

A

Dinosaurs’ mouths contained teeth; however, birds evolved toothless beaks over millions of years.

B

Mei long, a duck-sized bipedal dinosaur from the Cretaceous era, was found preserved in volcanic ash.

C

Birds’ bones are hollow, a trait that was shared by many dinosaur species.

D

Birds are descended from the theropod group of dinosaurs, which contained Tyrannosaurus rex.

Similarity Example · Matching Process

Scan for similarity language

  • The question requires words indicating that modern birds and dinosaurs are alike in some way.
  • Any choice indicating difference, such as although or whereas, will be wrong.
  • In choice A, however signals contrast, so A is incorrect.
  • In choice C, shared signals similarity, so C matches the focus.
  • Choice D mentions descent from dinosaurs, but it does not actually state a similarity.

Full Student Notes Example · Carrie Mae Weems

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes.

  • Carrie Mae Weems (born 1953) is a photographer and installation artist whose works employ text, fabric, audio, digital images and video.
  • She has received major awards from the MacArthur Foundation and the American Academy in Rome.
  • Her 2021 installation in Chicago featured photos, video, text, and furniture.
  • Visitors were encouraged to browse and sit at desks in a recreation of a historic classroom.
  • The same year, her “Cyclorama” exhibit at the New York City Armory included video projections, shadow puppets, and a voice-over narration.
▣ Mark for Review

The student wants to emphasise the variety of materials employed by Weems to an audience unfamiliar with her work. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to achieve this goal?

A

Carrie Mae Weems’s artwork has been recognised by both the MacArthur Foundation and the American Academy in Rome.

B

In 2021, Weems staged an installation in Chicago in which visitors were encouraged to participate directly in a recreation of a historic classroom.

C

Carrie Mae Weems, who is considered among the most influential contemporary American artists, employs a diverse set of media: her shows include elements ranging from photos to furniture to shadow puppets.

D

Carrie Mae Weems’s 2021 show in New York City, unlike her show in Chicago the same year, included shadow puppets as well as a voice-over narration.

Weems Example · What the Answer Must Do

Key requirements

  • It must emphasise the variety of materials used by Weems.
  • Because the audience is unfamiliar with Weems’s work, it must provide basic background explaining who she is.
  • That means the correct answer will probably be on the longer side.

Why C matches

Choice C includes background information and directly matches the idea of variety through “a diverse set of media” and examples such as photos, furniture, and shadow puppets.

Weems Example · Why the Others Do Not Match

Elimination

  • A gives context about awards, but says nothing about materials.
  • B refers to one installation only and lacks background information about Weems.
  • D mentions different materials, but does not introduce Weems to an unfamiliar audience.
  • Therefore, the answer is C.

Full Student Notes Example · Wolves

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes.

  • Wolves produce a range of vocalisations, including growls, whines, and barks.
  • To show submission, a wolf pulls its tail between its legs.
  • A raised tail indicates dominance.
  • Wolves have powerful scent receptors and can perceive odours a mile or more away.
  • Observers may mistake some of wolves’ play behaviours for aggression.
▣ Mark for Review

The student wants to present the importance of body language in wolves’ communication. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to achieve this goal?

A

Wolves display a variety of physical and vocal signals when interacting with one another.

B

The position of a wolf’s tail and ears can reveal its status relative to other animals in the pack.

C

Some wolf behaviours may appear aggressive to observers but are actually types of play.

D

Wolves are extremely effective hunters because they can smell prey from far away.

Wolves Example · Answer Logic

Focus = body language

  • Choice A refers to physical and vocal signals, but does not give actual examples of how wolves use their bodies.
  • Vocal signals do not count as body language.
  • Choice B mentions the position of a wolf’s tail and ears, a specific example of bodily communication.
  • Choice B is a much stronger answer and matches the required focus directly.
  • Choices C and D are off-topic.
